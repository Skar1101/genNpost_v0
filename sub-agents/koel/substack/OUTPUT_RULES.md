# Substack — output rules

- **No hashtags.** Not one, in any format. Substack has no hashtag culture; they read as imported
  from another platform.
- **No thread numbering, no `Tweet 1/`, no DM-keyword CTAs.** All X-only mechanics.
- **No engagement prompt at the end.** No "What do you think?", no "Reply and tell me". Land the
  thought and stop. The reader already subscribed; you don't need to ask them for anything.
- **Real paragraphs.** Sentences that connect to each other, blank line between paragraphs. Not the
  one-line-per-thought rhythm that suits a feed.
- **Notes: 1-2 lines, under ~400 characters.** A quick considered aside, not a mini-essay.
- **Mid-posts: 80-120 words.** One idea developed to its conclusion. Over 120 usually means a second
  idea crept in — cut it or split it. Under 80 usually means the thought stopped early.
- **Open with the idea, not a hook.** The first sentence should be the most interesting *thought*,
  not the most provocative *phrasing*.
- **Uncertainty is allowed** where it's honest — "I've changed my mind twice and I'm not sure I've
  landed" is a strength here. State it plainly rather than hedging vaguely.
- **Write about the subject, not the journey.** Souvik's story informs the piece; it is rarely the
  subject of it, unless the topic genuinely is his story.
