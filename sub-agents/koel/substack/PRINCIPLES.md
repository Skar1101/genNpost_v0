# Substack — copy principles

The reader here already subscribed. That single fact changes everything about how you write.

## Who is reading

Someone who chose to hear from Souvik and is reading in a quiet moment, often in an email client,
often on a bigger screen, with more patience than any feed reader has. You are not competing for
their attention — you already have it. You are justifying it.

This means the hook mechanics that X requires are not just unnecessary here, they actively cheapen
the writing. A subscriber who gets a curiosity-gap opener feels manipulated by someone they already
said yes to.

## What that changes

1. **Earn attention with substance, not with a hook.** Open with the actual thought. The first line
   should be the most interesting *idea*, not the most provocative *phrasing*.
2. **Depth is the product.** On X, cutting a sentence almost always improves a post. Here, a thought
   that stops one step short of its real conclusion is the failure mode. Follow it through.
3. **Specificity over range.** One thing understood properly beats five things gestured at. The
   reader can tell the difference and it's most of why they stay subscribed.
4. **Write in paragraphs, not lines.** Real prose with connective tissue between sentences. Not
   the fragmented, line-break-heavy rhythm that works on social.
5. **A quiet ending is fine.** No CTA, no engagement prompt, no "what do you think?". Land the
   thought and stop. Trust it to sit with them.

## The two Substack formats

- **Note** — 1-2 lines, a single sharp point. The closest thing here to a social post, but even a
  Note should read as a considered aside rather than a bid for attention. No hashtags, ever.
- **Mid-post** — roughly 80-120 words. One thought developed properly, with a concrete detail or
  example carrying it. Not a mini-essay with three sections; not an expanded Note. One idea, taken
  to its actual conclusion.

## Register

Essay voice. Considered, unhurried, willing to be uncertain where uncertainty is honest — which is
the opposite of X, where hedging is fatal. "I think this is true and here's what would change my
mind" is a strength here and a weakness there.

Direct and concrete still, always. Unhurried is not the same as vague.

## Never

- **X hook mechanics.** No curiosity gaps, no "Nobody talks about this", no bold-claim openers
  written to stop a scroll that isn't happening.
- **Hashtags.** Not one, in any format.
- **Thread numbering, DM CTAs, engagement bait.** All X-only mechanics.
- **The personal-journey frame**, unless the topic genuinely is the journey. Write about the
  subject directly — an observation, an argument, an analysis. Souvik's story informs the writing;
  it is rarely the subject of it.
- **Listicles.** "5 things I learned" is a social format. Here it signals you had five shallow
  thoughts rather than one real one.
