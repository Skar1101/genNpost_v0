# Substack — patterns that work

**Shape references only.** Learn the pacing and where the specificity sits. Never reuse the wording
or the subject.

The thing to notice across all of these: none of them open with a hook. They open with the thought.

---

## Notes (1-2 lines)

> The best code review comment I've ever received was three words: "why not simpler?" I've since
> asked it maybe two hundred times and it has never once been unwelcome.

> Every tool that promises to save an hour a week costs about an hour a week to keep working. The
> ones worth keeping are the ones you'd notice were gone within a day.

> Nobody's productivity system survives their first genuinely bad week. That's not a flaw in the
> system, but it is the only real test of one.

**What these do:** a specific, real observation, stated plainly. No hook, no hashtags, no CTA. Each
would be a worse post with "Here's what I learned:" in front of it.

---

## Mid-post — Pattern 1: the developed observation

> There's a particular kind of exhaustion that comes from work that's going fine.
>
> Not the exhaustion of a bad week, which at least gives you something to point at. The other one,
> where every individual day was reasonable and you still finish the month with nothing left. It's
> almost always the cost of decisions rather than the cost of work — thirty small judgement calls a
> day, none of them hard, all of them yours.
>
> Teams talk endlessly about reducing workload and almost never about reducing the number of
> decisions a person has to hold. A default, written down once, buys back more energy than an extra
> pair of hands.

**~120 words.** One idea, followed all the way through. The last line is a real conclusion, not a
summary of the preceding lines.

---

## Mid-post — Pattern 2: the honest uncertainty

> I've changed my mind about AI code review twice this year and I'm not confident I've landed.
>
> The first version was obvious: it caught nothing a linter wouldn't and generated enough noise that
> people stopped reading the comments. The second version, with the whole diff and the surrounding
> files as context, caught two real bugs in a month. That's not nothing. It's also not the step
> change the tooling is sold as.
>
> What I'd say now is narrower than what I'd have said in January. It's good at the class of mistake
> that comes from not having read the rest of the file, and useless at the class that comes from not
> having understood the problem. Most of the bugs I care about are the second kind.

**~130 words.** Uncertainty stated openly — which would be fatal on X and is a strength here.
Concrete numbers ground it. No CTA; it simply stops.

---

## What none of these do

No hashtags. No hooks. No "Here's the thing nobody tells you". No thread numbering. No engagement
prompt at the end. No listicles. No opener written to survive a scroll.
