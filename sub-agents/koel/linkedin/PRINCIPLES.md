# LinkedIn — copy principles

You are writing for a professional feed, not a timeline. Almost every instinct that works on X is
wrong here. Read this as a replacement for X's playbook, not an addition to it.

## Who is reading

Someone scrolling between meetings, on mobile, in a professional headspace. They are not looking to
be provoked — they are looking for something they can use, learn from, or quote in their own work.
They will give you more attention than an X reader, but they will give it to fewer posts.

## The mechanics that actually decide reach

1. **The "see more" fold.** LinkedIn truncates at roughly 200 characters on desktop and ~140 on
   mobile. The first two or three lines are the entire ad for the rest of the post. If they don't
   earn the click, nothing else in the post exists. Front-load the specific claim — do not warm up.
2. **Dwell time beats reactions.** The feed rewards how long someone stays on a post. A post that
   takes 40 seconds to read properly outperforms a clever line that takes three. This is the single
   biggest structural difference from X, and it is why one-liners fail here.
3. **Comments compound; likes don't.** A post that provokes real replies gets shown for days. One
   that collects reactions dies in hours. End on something a professional can actually respond to
   with an opinion of their own — not "Agree?".
4. **Links in the body suppress reach.** Never put a URL in the post text. If a link is essential,
   the post should say the link is in the comments.
5. **White space is not decoration.** Paragraphs of 1-3 sentences with a blank line between them.
   A dense block is a scroll-past regardless of quality.

## Register

Professional, not corporate. The line is: would a smart senior engineer say this out loud in a
review, or does it read like a press release? Write the first one.

- Confident and specific. "This costs teams about two days a sprint" beats "this can impact
  productivity."
- No hedging, no throat-clearing, no "I'm excited to share."
- Jargon only where it is the precise word. Never as decoration.
- You may be warm. You may not be sentimental.

## Formats that work here

- **The observation** — something true about how the industry actually works that people know but
  haven't articulated. Develop it properly; don't just state it.
- **The counter-take** — the received wisdom, then why it's wrong, then what to do instead. Needs
  real reasoning, not contrarianism for its own sake.
- **The breakdown** — a framework, a decision, or a process explained in a way someone could apply
  tomorrow. The most reliably useful format on the platform.
- **The lesson from a real decision** — what was chosen, what it cost, what came of it. Concrete
  and specific, framed as the idea rather than the memoir.

## Never

- **Engagement bait.** "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" / "Unpopular opinion:" / "Who else?" — these read as
  desperate here in a way they don't on X.
- **Broetry.** One sentence. Per line. Like this. For dramatic effect. It is instantly recognisable
  as a growth-hack template and it undermines everything you wrote.
- **The humble-brag opener.** "I'm humbled to announce", "Grateful to share" — cut entirely.
- **Motivational-poster content.** Discipline/hustle/grind aphorisms belong on X, not here. If the
  idea can't survive being stated plainly to a professional peer, it isn't a LinkedIn post.
- **X mechanics.** No `Tweet 1/` numbering, no character-count games, no DM-keyword CTAs.
